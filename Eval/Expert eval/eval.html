Instructions for Legal Evaluators

You are requested to evaluate legal reasoning generated by a machine learning system.

For each case, you will be provided with:

  • Full Case Details – This contains all factual information regarding the events, persons, and objects relevant to the case.
  • Court’s Reasoning – The actual reasoning provided by the court in support of its decision. This serves as the reference standard.
  • Two Machine-Generated Justification – Two justifications generated by the system.

    Important: Your task is to evaluate both the system generated text based only on the quality, correctness, and alignment of the outcome and justification provided for that outcome generated by the system.

Evaluation Criteria

1. Factual Accuracy (FA)

  • Does the justification accurately reflect the reasoning adopted by the court?
  • Are key facts from the case details correctly referenced and interpreted?
  • Are relevant laws, statutes, and legal principles correctly understood and applied?
  • Does the reasoning avoid factual distortions or incorrect legal inferences?

This is the most important criterion.

2. Completeness & Coverage (C1)

  • Does the justification address all material facts and legal considerations relied upon by the court?
  • Are significant arguments, contextual elements, or statutory considerations omitted?
  • Does it meaningfully capture the core structure of the court’s reasoning?

3. Clarity & Coherence (C2)

  • Is the justification logically structured?
  • Are the connections between facts, legal principles, and conclusions clearly articulated?
  • Is the reasoning internally consistent and easy to follow?

4. Extra Fact Flag

Indicate whether the court’s reasoning relies on a factual element that is not present in the provided case details. This is to understand if the information regarding the case provided in the "Case Details" section itself was complete or not.

  • A “fact” refers strictly to concrete information about an event, person, object, or circumstance relevant to the case.
  • It does not include legal interpretations, logical deductions, or inferences derived from the existing facts.

Flag Guidelines:
🟥 → No new factual element appears in the court’s reasoning. (leave the checkbox empty)
✅ → At least one new factual element appears in the court’s reasoning. (Mark the checkbox)

Scoring Scale (For FA, C1, C2)

Score Range Interpretation
1–3 Highly inaccurate, major omissions or distortions, poorly structured reasoning.
4–6 Partially accurate but incomplete, missing important legal reasoning or context.
7–9 Largely accurate and well-structured, with minor omissions or inconsistencies.
10 Fully aligned with court reasoning, factually precise, comprehensive, and coherent.

Evaluation Guidelines

  • Use the court’s reasoning only as a reference to verify factual and inferential correctness.
  • Do not compare writing style or literary quality.
  • Evaluate the machine-generated justification independently.
  • Focus strictly on legal reasoning quality and alignment with the actual judgment.
  • Apply strict but fair standards.
